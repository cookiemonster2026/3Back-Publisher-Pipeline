# 3Back Minimum Viable Brand and Design Brief

## Purpose and Boundary

This brief guides the outward-facing expression of 3Back across website content and design. It is a decision filter for Douglas Shimp, Marcelo, Codex, and other authoring tools.

Use it to determine whether outward-facing work:

- Positions 3Back correctly

- Speaks to the intended audience

- Stays within the theory's boundaries

- Sounds like 3Back

- Looks consistent with the brand

This brief does not govern the internal development of Operational Grip. It is not an internal operating manual, complete content strategy, website architecture, copy deck, AI prompting handbook, detailed design system, or catalog of every concept 3Back might discuss.

## Brand Category

3Back is **The Team Execution Company**.

3Back is an execution diagnostic and operating partner, not a training vendor. It may enter through education, but it examines the actual work system and the conditions affecting execution.

Operational Grip is 3Back's proprietary operating lens, not the business category.

## Primary Audience

3Back speaks first to:

1.  Executive leaders accountable for organizational execution

2.  Operational and transformation leaders responsible for improving execution

Broader Audience

Team leaders and practitioners closest to the work also use and influence Operational Grip, but they do not control the brand’s positioning.

Operational Grip is for everyone in the organization, but the broadest audience does not control the brand.

## Core Problem

> *Organizations do not outgrow teams. They lose the boundaries that made teams effective.*

Weak boundaries degrade local control, decision ownership, adaptability, and accountable execution.

## Commercial Scope

Operational Grip examines the complete commercial execution loop:

> *Demand -\> organizational response -\> delivered value -\> market acceptance -\> revenue*

Revenue is evidence that the market accepted the organization's response to demand. It is the economic spine of the 3Back argument, not an outcome 3Back promises.

## Brand Promise

> *3Back helps organizations find where execution has lost grip and make bounded changes that reveal what to do next.*

This promises disciplined diagnosis and forward movement, not certainty or guaranteed outcomes.

## 3Back and Operational Grip

3Back is the company. Operational Grip is the lightweight operating lens through which 3Back diagnoses execution and guides bounded change.

> *Bound what you can, act locally, observe the effects, and learn.*

Operational Grip should not appear as a separate company, packaged methodology, certification system, or guaranteed formula.

## How 3Back Works

> *Evidence first. Preserve what works, identify the real constraint, and recommend bounded changes tied to demand and delivery.*

3Back examines:

- Intake and demand signals

- Decision rights and ownership

- Team and authority boundaries

- Work readiness and visibility

- Dependencies and coordination

- Quality and feedback

- Leadership intervention

- Whether teams can act locally with predictable downstream effects

### Operating Discipline

> *One bounded problem, one decision, one accountable owner, and one observable result at a time.*

This guards against diagnostic abundance outrunning sponsorship, attention, and the organization's capacity to act.

### How We Start

> *You do not need the perfect place to start. You need a bounded place where action will reveal the system.*

A useful starting point is bounded, connected to real demand, safe enough to change, and observable enough to learn from.

## Proof Standard

3Back proves its value through evidence from actual work:

> *The condition observed, the bounded change made, and the observable result.*

Case evidence should lead. Credentials, certifications, and methodology claims should remain secondary.

## Theory Boundary

Operational Grip is a carefully constrained theory of organizational execution.

It addresses observable mechanisms such as:

- Authority and boundaries

- Demand response

- Decision ownership

- Coordination

- Feedback

- Work visibility

- Downstream effects

It is not a theory of personality, motivation, culture, group behavior, or social psychology.

### Epistemic Boundary

Operational Grip does not require understanding the whole system. It asks whether people can make a bounded local change, observe its effects, and learn from what happens.

> *How would you know?*

Complexity and chaos are not always caused by weak boundaries. When people cannot reason or act locally without unpredictable global effects, however, weak or misplaced boundaries are a strong suspect.

## Industry Expression

3Back is industry-independent, but never domain-indifferent. It learns the work quickly enough to diagnose execution within its real context.

> *The domain changes. The need for Operational Grip does not.*

Outward-facing work may selectively show fintech, healthcare, pharmacy, defense, machinery, manufacturing, and other domains as evidence of range. Do not use them as a generic industry montage.

## Communication Posture

3Back is:

- Clear-eyed

- Bounded

- Practical

- Diagnostic before prescriptive

- Commercially grounded

- Collaborative without becoming deferential

- Willing to identify failure without dramatizing it

- Careful about certainty and universal answers

## Voice

The voice is:

- Serious, independent, and practical

- Direct and declarative

- Precise about definitions and distinctions

- Somewhat forensic

- Operational rather than academic

- Comfortable naming dysfunction

- Willing to use client language without surrendering independent judgment

- Occasionally blunt or humorous, but never cute or promotional

Metaphors should explain mechanisms, not decorate the message.

## Language Discipline

Lead with **team execution**, not transformation, agility, Agile, or Scrum.

Agile, Scrum, and related terms may appear when they accurately describe a service, credential, client environment, or historical context. They must not define 3Back or Operational Grip.

Prefer concrete operational language. Avoid inflated consultancy terms such as:

- Transformation journey

- Transformation expert

- Transition expert

- Change expert

- Unlock potential

- Empowerment

- Best practices

- Proven methodology

- Culture change

- Scalable solutions

Client terminology may be used when useful, but 3Back should retain independent judgment and avoid language that obscures the actual work.

## Positioning Exclusions

3Back should not present itself primarily as:

- An Agile or Scrum training company

- A methodology consultancy

- A general management consultancy

- A motivational or culture-change brand

- A social or organizational psychology theory

- A company claiming guaranteed answers or financial outcomes

## Content Direction

The website should emphasize:

- Well-Formed Teams

- Bounded authority

- Decision ownership

- Local control and adaptability

- Demand signals and demand silencers

- Inspectable work and feedback

- Minimal necessary coordination

- Failure patterns that weaken Operational Grip

- Commercial execution from demand through revenue

Operational Grip provides the outward commercial narrative. Dr. Dan's material supplies deeper intellectual structure without making the website read like a Scrum glossary.

## Desired First Impression

3Back should convey **quiet authority, operational depth, and a bias toward action**.

The immediate impression should be:

- Serious and credible

- Independent and clear-eyed

- Commercially grounded

- Disciplined without feeling bureaucratic

- Human without becoming soft or psychological

The site should not resemble a training company, conventional consultancy, software startup, academic institute, or aggressive industrial brand.

## Overall Visual Character

The visual character should be:

- Editorial rather than corporate

- Structured without feeling rigid

- Spacious, restrained, and confident

- Grounded in real work, boundaries, flow, and cause and effect

- Driven by strong typography and selective visuals

- Minimally decorative

The site should feel like a serious field guide to organizational execution, not a consulting brochure or software landing page.

## Typography

Use a modern sans-serif system with:

- Strong editorial hierarchy

- High readability for long-form content

- Restrained variation in weight and scale

- A mature, established character

Avoid typography that feels futuristic, industrial, playful, academic, or conventionally corporate.

The brief locks the typographic character, not an exact typeface. Compare two or three options in actual headlines, body copy, diagrams, and mobile layouts during visual design. Source Sans 3 is the first candidate to test. Avoid defaulting to Inter.

> *Clear enough for the work, distinctive enough for the thinking.*

## Color

Use:

- Warm white background

- Near-black or charcoal typography

- Muted slate for secondary elements

- One restrained amber or ochre accent for demand, attention, and action

Color should carry meaning, not decorate the page. Exact values will be chosen during visual design.

## Visual Approach

Use this hierarchy:

1.  Strong typography as the primary visual system

2.  Simple diagrams showing boundaries, flow, demand, and downstream effects

3.  Selective documentary-style industry photography as evidence of range

4.  Editorial illustration only when it explains a specific idea

> *Show the work and its mechanisms, not generic people performing business.*

Operational Grip cartoons can remain a distinct content series without defining the entire website.

## Tales of the Grip and Doomscroll

- **Tales of the Grip** is the named editorial cartoon series.

- **Doomscroll** is the continuous-feed experience for browsing it.

- Each cartoon exposes an observable failure in execution.

- Humor creates recognition without softening the diagnosis.

- A cartoon may identify failure without prescribing a solution.

- The series must not drift into generic workplace satire, ridicule, or cynicism.

- Its visual personality may be distinctive without controlling the entire 3Back identity.

> *We may not know the universal recipe for success, but we can often recognize how organizations lose their grip.*

## Visual Conventions to Avoid

- Generic consulting imagery, including handshakes, staged meetings, and smiling teams

- Abstract corporate waves, gradients, blobs, and decorative geometry

- Technology imagery that makes 3Back resemble a software company

- Heavy industrial styling that overstates toughness

- Framework diagrams that imply a fixed methodology

- Excessive motion, visual noise, or promotional effects

- Industry montages that claim breadth without showing meaningful work

- Cartoons or humor used outside a clear diagnostic purpose

> *Nothing should make 3Back appear more certain, formulaic, or theatrical than the work itself.*

## Voice Calibration Sample

An organization can look controlled because one capable person is holding the work together. That is the visible strength. The hidden weakness is that everyone else has learned to route judgment through that person.

Operational Grip asks a smaller question: can the role closest to the demand make a bounded change, observe its effects, and learn without requiring someone else to reconstruct the whole system?

3Back starts there. Find one bounded problem. Give one role the judgment to own. Make the change observable. The goal is not to promise the answer. It is to restore enough grip for the organization to discover what to do next.

This sample calibrates the voice. It is not prescribed website copy.
