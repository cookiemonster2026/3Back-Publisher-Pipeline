# 3Back Homepage Content Blueprint

**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Working draft for content review and implementation testing  
**Purpose:** Define what the 3Back homepage says, in what order, and what each section must accomplish.

## 1. Page Objective

The homepage must establish 3Back as **The Team Execution Company** and make the business condition it addresses understandable to a senior leader encountering the company for the first time.

The page should leave that visitor with four conclusions:

1. 3Back works on organizational execution, not generic transformation or training.
2. Weak or misplaced boundaries can disconnect demand, decisions, work, and results.
3. Operational Grip is the current lead lens through which 3Back diagnoses that condition.
4. 3Back begins with one bounded, observable problem rather than a broad transformation promise.

## 2. Audience and Reading Priority

3Back speaks first to:

1. Executive leaders accountable for organizational execution
2. Operational and transformation leaders responsible for improving execution

Team leaders and practitioners closest to the work are an important broader audience, but they do not control the homepage positioning.

The page must work for an executive scanning in less than one minute. Each section should answer one practical question before introducing the next.

## 3. Homepage Narrative

| Section | Visitor question | Page answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hero | What does 3Back address? | Execution loses grip when the boundaries that keep work local and accountable weaken. |
| Observable problem | What would that look like here? | Decisions centralize, dependencies spread, coordination becomes reporting, and feedback arrives too late. |
| Operational Grip | What is 3Back's point of view? | Bound what you can, act locally, observe the effects, and learn. |
| Commercial loop | Why does this matter commercially? | Execution must remain connected from demand through market acceptance and revenue. |
| Working approach | What would 3Back actually do? | Examine the real work, identify the constraint, and make one bounded change with an accountable owner and observable result. |
| Evidence | Has this produced anything real? | Show the condition observed, the bounded change made, and the observable result. |
| Body of work | Is this only about Operational Grip? | Operational Grip leads a broader 3Back body of work that includes Well-Formed Teams, Agility, Scrum, publications, and education. |
| Current thinking | Can I examine the thinking before engaging? | Offer substantive articles, publications, diagrams, and Tales of the Grip. |
| Closing action | What should I do next? | Bring one bounded execution problem and start a conversation. |

## 5. Homepage Content

### Section 1: Hero

**Purpose:** Name the execution problem, identify 3Back's category, and give the visitor one clear next action.

**Eyebrow**

> 3Back | The Team Execution Company

**Headline**

> Organizations do not outgrow teams.  
> They lose the boundaries that made teams effective.

**Supporting copy**

> When decisions, dependencies, and coordination spread without control, work slows, accountability diffuses, and demand gets lost. 3Back helps leaders find where execution has lost grip and make bounded changes that reveal what to do next.

**Primary action**

> Start a Conversation

**Secondary action**

> Explore Operational Grip

**Visual treatment**

- Lead with typography, not a conventional consulting hero image.
- Use warm paper as the field, charcoal for the claim, and amber only to signal attention or action.
- If photography is tested, use one documentary image tied to observable work. Do not use an industry montage, staged meeting, or generic team image.
- Do not rotate industries, claims, or headlines.

### Section 2: What Loss of Grip Looks Like

**Purpose:** Convert the thesis into conditions an executive can recognize.

**Headline**

> Execution can look busy while the organization is losing control of the work.

**Introductory copy**

> The problem is rarely a lack of activity. It is the growing distance between demand, decisions, work, and observable results.

**Diagnostic conditions**

**One person holds the tapestry**  
Too much authority, context, and sequencing dependency has collapsed into one decision-maker.

**Local work stops staying local**  
A small change creates unpredictable dependencies, rework, or downstream damage.

**Coordination becomes reporting**  
Meetings describe activity but do not route the cross-boundary work that teams cannot safely resolve alone.

**Demand is silenced**  
Bloated backlogs, status theater, delayed feedback, and excessive process weaken the signal from the customer, product, or environment.

**Closing line**

> These are not isolated process problems. They are evidence that the organization may be losing Operational Grip.

**Visual treatment**

- Use four restrained editorial blocks or one vertical diagnostic sequence.
- Keep each condition legible without interaction.
- Amber may mark the evidence signal, but should not become a decorative panel color.

### Section 3: Operational Grip

**Purpose:** Introduce the current lead idea as 3Back's operating lens, not a separate brand or fixed methodology.

**Eyebrow**

> Operational Grip

**Headline**

> Can the people closest to the work make a bounded change and predict its likely effect?

**Body copy**

> Operational Grip is the ability of a team or scaled system to make bounded local changes, observe the effects, and adjust without creating uncontrolled downstream damage.

> It is not total system understanding. It is enough actionable control to adapt intelligently while keeping work connected to purpose, quality, and value.

**Operating line**

> Bound what you can, act locally, observe the effects, and learn.

**Diagnostic question**

> How would you know?

**Visual treatment**

- Present this as a strong editorial interruption within the 3Back system.
- A simple boundary diagram may show local action, an observable boundary, and downstream effects.
- Do not create an Operational Grip logo, badge, certification mark, or framework wheel.

### Section 4: The Commercial Execution Loop

**Purpose:** Connect the organizational argument to commercial execution without promising revenue.

**Headline**

> Work matters only when it remains connected to demand and delivered value.

**Body copy**

> 3Back examines the complete commercial execution loop. The question is where signal weakens, ownership diffuses, or feedback arrives too late to guide action.

**Flow**

> Demand -> Organizational response -> Delivered value -> Market acceptance -> Revenue

**Supporting copy**

> Revenue is evidence that the market accepted the organization's response to demand. It is not a result 3Back guarantees. The work is to restore enough grip for the organization to see, act, and learn before demand disappears into activity.

**Visual treatment**

- Use a simple horizontal vector flow on desktop and a vertical sequence on mobile.
- Amber identifies demand, breaks in signal, or the next action. The diagram should not resemble a proprietary five-step methodology.
- Do not use generic growth charts, upward arrows, or financial promises.

### Section 5: How 3Back Works

**Purpose:** Make the engagement approach concrete and appropriately bounded.

**Headline**

> Evidence first. One bounded problem at a time.

**Introductory copy**

> 3Back examines the actual work system before prescribing a solution. We preserve what works, identify the real constraint, and recommend a change that can be owned, observed, and corrected.

**Working sequence**

**1. Observe the condition**  
Follow real demand, decisions, work, dependencies, feedback, and downstream effects.

**2. Bound the intervention**  
Choose one problem that is connected to real demand, safe enough to change, and visible enough to learn from.

**3. Place decision ownership**  
Give one accountable role the judgment needed to act within a clear authority boundary.

**4. Inspect the result**  
Observe what changed, what propagated, and what the organization can now see well enough to do next.

**Operating standard**

> One bounded problem, one decision, one accountable owner, and one observable result at a time.

**Visual treatment**

- Use a numbered editorial sequence, not a circular process diagram.
- Keep the sequence clearly diagnostic and iterative. It must not imply guaranteed progression or a packaged transformation program.

### Section 6: Evidence From Actual Work

**Purpose:** Demonstrate credibility through a real case rather than credentials or methodology claims.

**Eyebrow**

> Evidence From Actual Work

**Headline**

> Where execution regained grip

**Case structure for the implementation test**

**Client or domain:** [REAL EXAMPLE REQUIRED]  
**Condition observed:** [Describe the concrete execution condition.]  
**Bounded change made:** [Describe the smallest material intervention.]  
**Observable result:** [Describe what became visible, predictable, faster, safer, or more locally controllable.]  
**Commercial connection:** [State how the result affected demand response, delivery, market acceptance, or revenue, if supported by evidence.]

**Internal requirement**

Do not publish invented metrics, composite case claims, anonymous praise, or a result that cannot be substantiated. If a publishable case is unavailable during implementation, retain a clearly marked placeholder rather than replacing evidence with credentials.

**Visual treatment**

- Use one substantial case-evidence block.
- Show the three-part proof standard prominently: condition, bounded change, observable result.
- A real artifact, diagram, or documentary image may support the case if permission exists.

### Section 7: A Body of Work Built Around Execution

**Purpose:** Preserve 3Back's intellectual history without weakening Operational Grip as the current lead idea.

**Headline**

> Operational Grip is the current lead lens in a larger body of work.

**Body copy**

> 3Back has spent years examining how teams form, make decisions, adapt, coordinate, and deliver value. Operational Grip is the culmination of that work and the primary lens guiding 3Back now.

**Supporting areas**

**Well-Formed Teams**  
The bounded team and authority structure beneath accountable execution.

**Agility and Scrum**  
An important part of 3Back's intellectual history, education, and continuing practice where those terms accurately describe the work.

**Publications and learning**  
Books, articles, courses, and deeper material for leaders and practitioners who want to examine the ideas further.

**Boundary statement**

> 3Back is the enduring brand. Operational Grip leads the current argument. No single concept independently defines the company.

**Visual treatment**

- Keep Operational Grip visually primary.
- Present the supporting areas as restrained text links or editorial pathways, not equal product cards.
- Do not place certification logos or course catalogs on the homepage.

### Section 9: Closing Action

**Purpose:** Offer a low-theater, specific way to engage 3Back.

**Headline**

> Find one place where action will reveal the system.

**Body copy**

> You do not need the perfect place to start. Bring one execution problem where demand is real, ownership is unclear, or local action keeps creating downstream effects. We will examine whether it is bounded enough to act on and observable enough to learn from.

**Primary action**

> Start a Conversation

**Support line**

> No transformation pitch. No promise of a universal answer. Start with the work in front of you.

**Visual treatment**

- Use a charcoal field or strong near-white close with one amber action.
- Keep the form or destination out of the implementation test. Test only the copy, hierarchy, and action treatment.

## 7. Visual and Interaction Constraints

- Use the 3Back Visual Direction Tile v0.3 as the current visual constraint.
- Use Source Sans 3 as the implementation-test typeface.
- Use warm paper for the site field, near-white for sustained reading surfaces, charcoal for authority, muted slate for secondary information, and amber only for demand, evidence, and action.
- Keep all copy as live, selectable text. Do not flatten headings, diagrams, or navigation into images.
- Use real semantic heading order with one H1.
- Test desktop and mobile layouts.
- Keep navigation nonfunctional during the visual implementation test.
- Avoid auto-rotation, parallax, decorative motion, generic consulting photography, industry montages, gradients, and promotional effects.
- Use one strong documentary image only if it materially supports the page argument.
- Use diagrams to clarify boundaries, flow, cause and effect, or downstream consequences.

## 8. Content Still Required Before Publication

The blueprint is sufficient to build the homepage implementation test. Publication still requires:

1. One substantiated case-evidence block
2. The exact destination and process behind **Start a Conversation**
3. The current article or publication titles used in **Current Thinking**
4. A confirmed documentary image or a decision to use a typography-led homepage without one
5. Final navigation destinations and legal links
6. Final company contact information

These are bounded content gaps. They do not block the desktop and mobile implementation test.
